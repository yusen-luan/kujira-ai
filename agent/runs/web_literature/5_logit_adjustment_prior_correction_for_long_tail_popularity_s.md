The most reliably reproduced fix for popularity-skewed pointwise training (no randomized-exposure log needed) is **logit adjustment**: shift each example's logit by a term derived only from the empirical label/item marginal frequency (a prior you can estimate directly from the training log itself), not from any propensity/exposure model. Two variants exist: (1) **loss-based** — during training, add τ·log(π_y) to the raw logit before computing the (softmax or BCE) loss, so the model is forced to produce a larger margin for rare labels to compensate for the added positive offset on frequent ones; at inference the raw (unadjusted) logit is used directly for ranking, since the correction is already baked into the learned weights. (2) **post-hoc** — train normally, then at inference subtract τ·log(π_y) from the logit before scoring/ranking. τ≈1 corresponds to the Bayes-optimal correction under a long-tail generalization of the softmax/logistic model; τ<1 is used empirically when the plain prior over-corrects. For a pointwise BCE/CTR head this adapts naturally by treating π_i as each item's empirical positive-click marginal (frequency-based, not propensity-based) and applying a single scalar offset to the pre-sigmoid logit for that item during training loss computation only. Cost is negligible (one extra table lookup/add per example) and it requires no additional model capacity or exposure-randomization data — only stored item positive-frequency counts, which is exactly what's available in a KuaiRand-Pure-style Gini≈0.79 log. A closely related, recsys-native version of the same mechanism is the "logQ correction" used for sampled-softmax two-tower models (Yi et al., RecSys 2019, Google — subtracting log of the sampling/popularity probability from the training logit and using raw dot-products at serving time); it is not the paper behind this note's source_url but is worth naming since it's the standard adaptation of logit adjustment specifically for large-scale candidate ranking/retrieval. In practice, teams report logit adjustment beats plain inverse-propensity weighting or negative up/down-weighting on per-user ranking metrics (GAUC/nDCG) in popularity-skewed pointwise setups because it directly targets the score calibration that ranking metrics are sensitive to, rather than reweighting gradients, and it degrades gracefully when the estimated prior is only approximate.
