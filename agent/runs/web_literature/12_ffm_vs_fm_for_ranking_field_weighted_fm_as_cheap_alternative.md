FFM's advantage over plain FM (Juan et al. 2016) was established on pointwise CTR benchmarks (Criteo/Avazu, LogLoss/AUC) with dozens of fields; I found no reliable, citable evidence that FFM reliably beats FM on ranking-quality metrics (NDCG/Recall@K) under a pairwise BPR loss, and with only ~5 low-cardinality fields the field-interaction diversity that gives FFM its edge is much smaller anyway, so gains are not established for your setting. FFM's cost is O(n²k) field-specific embedding tables (n=fields, k=latent dim) per feature value, which is what causes the blowup — with only 5 fields this is a manageable 25 sub-vectors per feature-value in principle, but still 5x the memory of FM per high-cardinality feature (user_id, video_id, author_id) if done naively for every field pair. The cheap, literature-backed fix is not "make 2 fields field-aware and leave the rest shared" (I could not find that specific ablation validated anywhere) but Field-weighted FM (FwFM, Pan et al. 2018, WWW): keep one shared embedding per feature (like FM) and add a single learned scalar weight r_{F(i),F(j)} per field-pair to modulate the interaction strength, reproducing ~FFM-level accuracy with as little as ~4% of FFM's parameters and O(n²) extra scalars instead of O(n²k) extra vectors. A related paper, FM² (Field-matrixed FM, arXiv:2102.12994), similarly reduces cost via low-rank field-interaction matrices rather than full per-field embeddings, and is a reasonable named alternative to mention. Given the weak evidence for FFM's benefit here plus the small field count, FwFM (or plain FM) inside your BPR loop is the safer, CPU-tractable choice; the specific "field-aware only user_id/video_id" heuristic is a plausible engineering shortcut in the spirit of FwFM/FM² but should be treated as an untested ad hoc idea, not an established method.
